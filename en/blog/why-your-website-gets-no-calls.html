Why your website gets traffic but no calls

If your site gets visits but no calls, the problem is usually not design — it is the missing path for the customer. Five common causes and the fix for each.

A simple test: if a stranger opened your site right now, would they know within five seconds what you sell, who it is for and what to do next? For most sites we review the answer is no. There is traffic, but no calls. The reason is almost always one of these five.

1. The first screen is about you, not the customer

“With over a decade of experience…” means nothing to a visitor who still does not know which problem you solve. Your first line should state the outcome the customer wants, in their words. Compare “provider of comprehensive digital solutions” with “a website that gets you calls, live in six weeks”.

2. You have more than one primary button

When one page offers “contact us”, “download the catalogue”, “join the newsletter” and “browse products”, you have handed the decision to the visitor. Every page needs one job, with the other options clearly secondary.

3. It is slow on mobile

Most Iranian web traffic is mobile, often on a cellular connection. If a page takes more than three seconds, a large share of visitors leave before seeing anything. Uncompressed images, externally hosted fonts and heavy scripts are the usual culprits.

  • Export images at the size they are displayed, not at camera resolution.
  • Self-host your Persian font so you do not depend on external services.
  • Defer any script that is not needed for the first paint.

4. There are no trust signals

Before calling, Iranian customers look for a few things: reviews, case studies with detail, a real address and phone number, and — if you take online payments — a trust seal. Without them, the question “what if I pay and nothing happens?” stays unanswered, and people avoid the risk.

5. Your contact method is not the one they prefer

A ten-field form is a wall for someone browsing on a phone. WhatsApp, click-to-call and a three-field form usually outperform a long form several times over. Do not force customers to reach you your way.

Where to start

Before a full redesign, try this order: rewrite the top of the page, cut down to one primary button, fix mobile speed, then add reviews and case studies. The first three can usually be done in a week and make the biggest difference.

If you cannot say how many calls came from your site, your first problem is measurement, not design.

One last thing: put a conversion event on every contact button. Until you know which page produces calls, every change is a guess.
