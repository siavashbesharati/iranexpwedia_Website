Six low-capital business ideas — and how to test them

A good idea is one you can test before spending heavily. Six startable ideas and a two-week validation test.

Most small businesses fail not because of a bad idea but because of spending too early — rent, inventory and branding before anyone has proven they will pay. These are ideas you can start small.

1. A specialist service for one industry

Not “graphic designer” but “packaging designer for home-made food brands”. Narrow specialisation lowers competition and raises your rate. Startup cost is near zero and the first client usually comes from your existing network.

2. Information brokerage in a local market

Every market has a gap between people who have something and people looking for it: empty storage, unused production capacity, seasonal labour. Connect the two sides reliably and you have a business before you invest anything.

3. Subscriptions instead of one-off sales

Anything a customer needs repeatedly can become a subscription: site maintenance, monthly content, scheduled equipment servicing. Predictable revenue is the biggest advantage a small business can have.

4. Practical training in a provable skill

If you can do something others pay for, teaching it has a market. Starting with one small online workshop costs almost nothing and tells you whether the demand is real.

5. A local service that is simply dependable

In repairs, cleaning or maintenance, the top complaint is rarely quality — it is missed appointments and unanswered calls. A service that shows up on time and answers the phone wins its neighbourhood.

6. Focused selling on an existing platform

Instead of building a store, start with a narrow category on an existing marketplace. Build the site and the brand once sales are steady — not before.

A two-week test before you spend

  1. Write the idea in one sentence: for whom, which problem, at what price.
  2. Build one simple page with that sentence and one way to contact you.
  3. Get in front of twenty real prospects — not friends — and show them the page.
  4. Ask whether they will pay a deposit. “Interesting” means no.
  5. If at least two pay, the idea is alive. If nobody does, change the offer or the audience — not the page design.

The test costs a few days and almost no money, but it prevents the most common mistake: building something nobody asked for.
